# Team Minik (Michelle Minik) — Meeting Prep

## TL;DR — your one goal
You are NOT there to sell her your agent-service (she sells her own). You are there to find
out **why she wanted to talk**, and to land ONE prize: **become her agent-RECRUITMENT engine.**
Team Minik grows by recruiting agents — and your machine already cold-reaches real-estate
agents at scale, which is exactly who she recruits. You fill her recruiting calendar; she
closes. (Seller-lead outbound for her agents is the *secondary* angle.) Everything else is
intel. Walk in **curious and guarded.**

## Who she is (read the power dynamic honestly)
- Michelle Minik. 25 years, **1,534 deals, $354M** sold, Today Show w/ Barbara Corcoran,
  Fox 10. Goodyear, AZ. Team of **~16–23 agents across 22+ states** under Realty of America.
- Her business = **recruiting agents + selling them leads, coaching, tech, revenue-share**
  (85/15 split, ~$14k cap). Her customers are AGENTS. She does a version of what you do.
- She is bigger, more established, media-credentialed. Be **confident, not deferential, not
  boastful.** You have something she likely lacks (see wedge). Treat it as peer-to-peer.

## Reality check on this meeting
- You cold-emailed HER 3× (last one June 1, "the problem with bought leads"). She **opened
  all 3, replied to none.** No meeting exists in your system → this was set up off-channel.
- Translation: **she is not a warm buyer.** She is curious / scoping / has her own agenda.
  Do not walk in assuming she wants to buy. **Find out what she wants first.**

## Why she's probably meeting you — 3 scenarios + the tell
1. **Scouting a new competitor** (most likely). She got pitched by a new lead-gen player and
   wants to size you up / see if you're a threat or worth copying.
   *Tell:* lots of "how do you do it?" questions, little about her needs.
   *Play:* share outcomes, never mechanics. Flip it — ask about HER business.
2. **Recruiting you / absorbing the capability.** She recruits for a living; she may try to
   fold Aureon (or you) into her team for a split.
   *Tell:* "you should come under our umbrella," "we could give you leads + equity."
   *Play:* polite deflect → "I run Aureon as its own thing; open to a partnership, not a split."
3. **Genuine partnership** (the prize). She wants more listings for her agents and you can
   supply seller appointments.
   *Tell:* she talks about her agents needing more seller/listing volume.
   *Play:* lean in — propose a small paid pilot for 3–5 of her agents.

## Your wedge (what makes you worth her time)
**PRIMARY — you already reach her recruiting target.** Team Minik grows by recruiting agents.
Your machine already cold-reaches **real-estate agents at scale** — the exact people she
recruits. You can fill her recruiting calendar with zero lift on her side. She even already
saw your outreach quality (she opened all 3 of your emails). The line:
> "You recruit agents for a living. My machine already reaches real-estate agents at scale —
> I can fill your recruiting calendar with agents who want better splits, leads and coaching.
> You close them; I book them."

**SECONDARY — the seller side.** Everyone sells BUYER leads; you do done-for-you SELLER /
listing outbound (the harder, higher-value lane) for her agents' pipelines. Plus you can run
**referral-partner outreach** (lenders, attorneys, builders, relocation, investors) to feed
her team deals.

All three are *outcomes* — none reveals the *how*.

## Open with discovery (don't pitch first)
1. "Before I say anything about us — what made you want to connect?" *(uncovers her intent)*
2. "Tell me about the team — where are your agents strongest, and where do they want more?"
3. "How are your agents getting listings right now vs buyers?" *(surfaces the seller gap)*
4. "What does your lead system give them today — and what's still a pain?"
Let her talk 60–70% of the meeting. You learn her gaps AND her intent before committing.

## The partnership pitch (when the door opens)
**Lead with recruiting (the prize):**
> "Here's the cleanest way we could work together: I point my outreach at agents in your
> markets with your recruiting offer — your credibility, your splits, your leads — and I book
> qualified recruiting calls straight onto your team's calendar. You stay the closer; I'm the
> top-of-funnel engine. We prove it on a small paid pilot, then scale."
Then stop and let her react. Propose: **30-day pilot, one market, performance-based (you pay
per booked recruiting call).**

**Have the seller angle ready as the second lane:** "Separately, for the agents you already
have, I can run seller/listing outbound so they get listing appointments that are theirs
alone." (Land-and-expand: recruiting first, then seller-leads + referral-partner outreach.)

## SHARE vs PROTECT (the guardrails)
SHARE (outcomes, credibility):
- That you do done-for-you seller/listing outbound for agents and book appointments.
- General results framing ("agents get listing appointments that are theirs alone").
- Willingness to prove it on a small pilot.

PROTECT (your moat — do NOT reveal):
- The **give-first attorney-referral-list lead magnet** (your hook). Never mention it.
- Your **infrastructure**: subdomains, warmup, Resend, the tech stack, automation.
- Your **client list / who you work with**, volumes, pricing internals.
- How leads are sourced/scraped. If pressed: *"That's our IP — happy to show results, not the recipe."*

## Handling her likely lines
| She says | You say |
|---|---|
| "How do you actually generate the leads?" | "That's our engine / IP. I'd rather show you results than the recipe — want to run a small pilot?" |
| "We already do lead gen." | "For buyers, mostly — everyone is. We specialize in the seller/listing side, which is the harder lane. Different problem." |
| "You should come join my team / come under us." | "I appreciate that — I run Aureon as its own company. Where I see fit is a partnership: we feed your agents listings, you stay the brokerage." |
| "What does it cost?" | "Depends on scope — let's first see if it produces listings on a small pilot, then talk numbers." |
| "Send me everything about how it works." | "I'll send a short results overview. The mechanics are our IP, but a pilot will show you everything that matters — the listings." |

## Traps to avoid
- Don't over-explain to impress her — every mechanic you reveal, she can rebuild.
- Don't accept a "split/join" framing that turns your scalable business into one of her agents.
- Don't agree to anything on the call — "let me put a 1-pager together" buys you control.
- Don't disparage portals/competitors by name; stay above it.

## Close / next step to propose
- If partnership interest: "I'll send a one-page pilot proposal — 3–5 agents, 30 days, one
  market — and we pick a start date." (You control the follow-up artifact.)
- If just scouting: "Good to connect — if your agents ever want more listings, you know where
  I am." Keep the door open, give nothing away.

## 2-minute pre-call checklist
- [ ] Decide your walkaway: a split/recruitment offer is a NO; a paid pilot is a YES.
- [ ] Have the seller-specialist one-liner ready (memorize the wedge sentence).
- [ ] Have 3 discovery questions on a sticky note.
- [ ] Resolve to talk <40% of the time.
- [ ] Mentally rehearse the "that's our IP" deflection so it's calm, not defensive.
