# The Hormozi Lead Magnet Formula

A working design brief and grading rubric. Grounded in *$100M Leads* (2023) and *$100M Offers* (2021). Where I name a framework (the Value Equation, MAGIC naming, the three lead magnet types), it is Hormozi's own. I have not added concepts he did not teach.

## 1. What a great lead magnet is

Hormozi's definition: a lead magnet is **a complete solution to a narrow problem**. It is a free or low-cost thing you give a stranger that fully solves one small, specific problem they have right now.

Two words carry the whole definition:

- **Complete.** It actually delivers a result. The person finishes consuming it and is genuinely better off, not left with "now go buy the rest to get any value." A half-solution makes you look like a tease and burns trust.
- **Narrow.** It solves *one* problem for *one* avatar. Not "grow your business." Solve "write a cold email that gets a reply."

Why narrow-and-complete beats broad-and-partial: a complete solution to a small problem proves you can solve the big one. The prospect reasons, "If the free thing worked this well, the paid thing must be excellent." A broad-and-partial magnet does the reverse: it demonstrates that your help is incomplete, so the prospect assumes the paid offer is also incomplete. Narrow also wins on the Value Equation (Section 2): a narrow problem can be solved fast and with little effort, so perceived value goes up. Broad means slow and effortful, so perceived value drops. Completeness is what earns the right to ask for the next step.

## 2. The Value Equation

```
            Dream Outcome  ×  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
   Value =  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                  Time Delay  ×  Effort & Sacrifice
```

Raise the top two, lower the bottom two. A lead magnet must score high on all four or it will not pull.

- **Dream Outcome (maximise):** Name the specific end result the prospect already wants, and make the magnet deliver a visible slice of it. "Lose 5 pounds in 7 days," not "be healthier."
- **Perceived Likelihood (maximise):** Stack proof inside the magnet itself: real screenshots, real numbers, before/afters, a worked example they can copy. The prospect should believe "this will work for me," not just "this worked for someone."
- **Time Delay (minimise):** Engineer a win in minutes, not days. A checklist used once, a template filled in immediately, a calculator that outputs now. The first useful result should land on first contact.
- **Effort & Sacrifice (minimise):** Strip steps. The prospect should reach the result with almost no work, no new skills, and nothing to install or learn. "Fill in three boxes and copy the output," not "complete this 12-part workbook."

## 3. The three types

Hormozi gives three ways to build the magnet. Pick the one that matches where the prospect is.

1. **Reveal a problem (diagnosis).** Show the prospect a problem they did not know they had, or did not know the size of. This creates urgency because you cannot un-see a problem. Use when the prospect is unaware or complacent and your paid offer fixes what the diagnosis exposes. *Example:* a free website speed audit that scores their site and lists what is slowing it down. Revealing the broken thing creates demand for the fix.

2. **Free sample or trial.** Give a limited taste of the actual paid thing: a sample, a free session, a time-limited or feature-limited trial. Use when the product is genuinely good and experiencing it is the fastest path to belief, and when you can cap the sample so it satisfies but does not replace the paid version. *Example:* one free coaching call, or a 7-day software trial.

3. **One step of a multi-step process.** Solve completely the first step of a process whose full result needs the remaining steps. Use when your paid offer is naturally a sequence and step one produces a real, standalone win. *Example:* free teeth whitening from a dentist (a complete win) that reveals the need for ongoing dental care, or a free meal plan that is step one of full coaching.

## 4. How to build one

Hormozi's build sequence, as a checklist. Do these in order.

1. **Pick the narrow problem and the exact avatar.** One problem, one type of person. If you cannot state it in one sentence, it is too broad.
2. **Choose how you solve it.** Select one of the three types in Section 3 based on where the prospect is.
3. **Choose the delivery format.** Software/tool, information (checklist, template, guide, video), a service, or a physical item. Pick whatever delivers the win with the least Time Delay and Effort.
4. **Make it worth paying for.** Build it to a standard where the prospect would have happily paid for it. Hormozi's bar: deliver so much value it feels like a steal that it was free. This is the 5x value rule applied to a free thing.
5. **Make it fast and easy to consume.** Cut every nonessential step. Get the prospect to the win quickly and with minimal effort (lower the Value Equation denominator).
6. **Make sure it actually delivers the win.** It must produce the promised result, not just promise it. Over-deliver here; this is where trust is earned.
7. **Attach a clear call to action to the next step.** End with the single, specific next action toward the paid offer (see Section 5). No win without a next step; no next step without a win.

## 5. The bridge to the paid offer

The magnet and the paid offer must be on the same path. Solving the narrow problem should **reveal or create the next problem** that only the paid (ideally recurring) offer solves. Consuming the magnet should leave the prospect *more* aware of what they still need, not satisfied and gone.

Design rule: the magnet's win is step one; the paid offer is steps two onward, or the maintenance of the win. The free diagnosis exposes a problem the paid service fixes. The free first step works so well the prospect wants the whole process. The free sample is good enough that the prospect wants the full, ongoing version.

Guard against two failure modes:

- **The magnet is too complete in the wrong direction.** If it fully solves the *entire* problem your paid offer solves, you have given away the business and created no next problem. Solve the narrow problem completely, but make sure a larger, adjacent problem becomes visible.
- **The magnet is unrelated to the offer.** A magnet that attracts people who do not want your paid offer produces leads who never buy. The bridge must be a straight line: same avatar, same domain, next problem.

Favor a next problem that recurs, because recurring problems map to recurring revenue. The best bridge ends with the prospect thinking "I want this handled for me, continuously."

## 6. Naming

Hormozi's rule: the name is **specific, benefit-led, and states the result**. It is built after the value, not before. Use the MAGIC structure from *$100M Offers*: **M**ake a magnetic reason why, **A**nnounce the avatar, **G**ive them a goal (the result), **I**ndicate a time interval, **C**omplete with a container word (Checklist, Guide, Plan, Audit, Toolkit, Blueprint, Masterclass). Name the avatar, name the result, name the time, and wrap it in a container word.

Two title patterns:

- **The [time interval] [container] for [avatar] to [specific result]**, e.g. "The 5-Minute Checklist for Coaches to Book 10 Sales Calls a Week."
- **Free [container]: [specific result] in [time interval] without [pain]**, e.g. "Free 7-Day Meal Plan: Lose 5 Pounds Without the Gym."

If the title does not name the result, it is not done.

## 7. Scoring rubric

Grade any lead magnet 0 to 10 on each criterion. Multiply by the weight, sum, and divide by 100 for a weighted score out of 10.

| # | Criterion | Weight | What a 10 looks like |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| 1 | Narrow and complete | 20% | Solves exactly one problem for one avatar, and fully delivers that result. Stated in one sentence. Nothing missing to get the win. |
| 2 | Dream outcome | 12% | Names a specific result the avatar already wants and delivers a visible slice of it. The prospect pictures the outcome before consuming. |
| 3 | Perceived likelihood / proof | 12% | Real proof embedded: screenshots, numbers, before/afters, a worked example. Prospect believes "this will work for me." |
| 4 | Speed to value (Time Delay) | 12% | First useful win on first contact, in minutes. No setup wall before any payoff. |
| 5 | Low effort and sacrifice | 10% | Win reached with almost no work, no new skills, nothing to install. Steps few and explicit. |
| 6 | Worth paying for (5x value) | 12% | So good the prospect is surprised it was free. Would have paid for it. |
| 7 | Bridge to paid offer | 14% | Solving it reveals or creates the next (ideally recurring) problem the paid offer solves. Same avatar, straight line, ends with one specific CTA. |
| 8 | Name (MAGIC) | 8% | Title is specific and benefit-led: names avatar, result, time interval, and a container word. |

**Pass bar:** Ready to ship at a **weighted total of 8.0 or higher**, with **no single criterion below 6**, and criteria 1 (narrow and complete) and 7 (bridge to paid offer) each **at 7 or higher**. Below 8.0, or any hard gate missed, send it back. The two gated criteria are non-negotiable because a magnet that is not complete teaches the prospect you give incomplete help, and a magnet with no bridge produces leads who never buy.
