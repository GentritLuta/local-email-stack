01 · The real problem
Clicks are not appointments

Most real estate ad money buys clicks. Almost none of it buys listings.

A cheap cost-per-click feels like winning. It is not. A seller lead has to survive four links to turn into an appointment on your calendar, and the appointment is usually lost in the three links after the ad, the ones nobody optimises.

80%
of seller leads are never properly followed up.
Speed-to-lead decides it: a lead contacted in 5 minutes is many times more likely to convert than one contacted in an hour. Most agents take hours, or never call at all. This is the cheapest appointment you are leaving on the table.

The four links, and where each one leaks

1

Creative — stop a seller mid-scroll

A homeowner is not looking for an agent. The first line and the first image have one job: pattern-interrupt with a neighborhood, a number, or a question. Most ads lead with the agent's face and brokerage, which a seller scrolls straight past.
2

Offer — the single biggest lever

Not "thinking of selling?" Give a reason to raise a hand now: a specific home valuation, a list of recent sales on their street, a what-your-home-would-net estimate. The offer, not the budget, is what decides your lead cost.
3

Funnel — click to booking, with zero friction

One promise, one form, one next step. Every extra field and every slow page sheds leads. The appointment is won or lost in the ten seconds after the click.
4

Follow-up — the 80% everyone leaves

A booked call needs a reminder sequence. A form-fill needs a call in minutes and a multi-touch follow-up over days. This is where the listing is actually made.

02 · What I see in almost every account
The leaks, named

Seven money-losing mistakes

Open almost any agent's account and at least four of these are live right now. Each one is an appointment walking out the door.

The ad leads with you, not the seller. Your face and brokerage in line one. Sellers scroll past anything that looks like an ad for an agent.
One creative, never tested. A healthy account runs at least three angles at once and kills the losers. One ad means you are guessing.
A vague offer ("thinking of selling?"). No reason to act now. The offer is the lever, and a weak one makes every click expensive.
The landing page asks for too much. Long forms and slow pages shed leads in the first ten seconds.
No speed-to-lead. Leads contacted hours later, or never. Five minutes versus one hour is a different business.
No follow-up sequence. One call, then silence. 80% of the value is in the next five touches.
Optimising for clicks, not booked calls. Cheap clicks, zero listings. You get what you tell the algorithm to buy.

03 · Score your setup
12-point teardown

Run your own account against this

Open your live ad and your landing page in another tab. Answer each honestly with a yes or a no. Every no is an appointment leaking out.

Creative
First line names a neighborhood or a number, not your name.
At least three creative variations running right now.
The image stops the scroll on its own, sound off.
Offer
A specific reason to raise a hand today (valuation, comps, net estimate).
The offer is the headline, not a "thinking of selling?".
It is local, named to the area you actually work.
Funnel
One promise, one form, one next step, no detours.
Form asks for the minimum (name, contact, address).
Page loads under 3 seconds on a phone.
Follow-up
New leads are contacted within 5 minutes, automatically.
A multi-touch sequence runs for at least 7 days.
You optimise to booked calls, not clicks or cost-per-lead.
